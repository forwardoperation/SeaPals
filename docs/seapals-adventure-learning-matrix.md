# SeaPals Adventure Learning Matrix

**Status:** Phase 0 planning baseline

**Audience assumption:** Ages 8-12; core dialogue targets an approximately grades 4-6 reading level, with optional glossary detail.
**Review gate:** Every chapter requires approval from a qualified marine-science educator or subject-matter expert before final dialogue, art labels, measurements, or interventions ship.

The game uses an `observe -> understand -> act -> reflect -> recall` pattern. Duels demonstrate SeaPals readiness and earn community trust; they do not imply that an ecosystem problem was defeated.

| Town | Settlement and habitat | Target concept | Misconception to address | Evidence and player decision | Modest consequence and later callback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shellshore Academy** | Island harbor and lagoon | A habitat is a place an organism lives; an ecosystem includes organisms, conditions, and relationships. | Habitat and ecosystem are two names for exactly the same thing. | Compare organisms and physical conditions at two survey points, then identify a safe marked boat route. | The mentor approves a first voyage. Sunpatch asks the player to reuse the habitat/ecosystem distinction. |
| **Sunpatch Cay** | Coral-reef island | Bleaching is a stress response; field observers describe lesions and gather evidence before assigning a disease diagnosis. | Every white coral is dead, or any visible white area can be diagnosed as disease by sight. | Compare healthy, pale, tissue-loss, and algae-covered patches; record temperature, water clarity, and repeat images; choose a reporting and local stress-reduction response. | Monitoring and no-anchor protection improve, but recovery is gradual and uncertain. Kelpwatch later recalls interacting heat and food-web stress. |
| **Brackwater Landing** | Floating/stilt estuary and mangrove town | Salinity, turbidity, and oxygen naturally vary; excess runoff or nutrients can create harmful patterns. | Murky estuary water is always polluted. | Compare readings across locations, tides, and rainfall; decide whether the pattern is expected variation or needs a source response. | Residents target a supported source while preserving productive nursery habitat. Current Commons asks where water and material may move next. |
| **Current Commons** | Open-ocean flotilla | Currents connect locations, and derelict gear can continue trapping wildlife; prevention and removal work together. | A single cleanup prevents future ghost gear. | Combine dated loss reports, tracked drifter positions, remote wildlife overlap, and repeated accumulation; map a likely search corridor, report it, and choose safe authorized response plus prevention. | Trained responders address the documented hazard while the flotilla strengthens loss prevention and reporting. Trenchlight recalls how currents transport material and food. |
| **Kelpwatch Island** | Rocky kelp-forest island | Kelp forms habitat, and changes among predators, grazers, and kelp can cascade through a food web. | Removing one species always restores balance. | Compare kelp cover, grazer abundance, and predator observations; build a three-link food web; choose a bounded monitoring/restoration response. | The town tests a response and keeps monitoring. Champion's Wake presents a different food-web constraint. |
| **Trenchlight Station** | Floating platform over a deep trench | Deep life is shaped by darkness, cold, pressure, and limited food; bioluminescence can serve different functions. | Every trench contains a vent and all deep life depends on chemosynthesis. | During an NPC-piloted sub descent, track light and pressure, photograph marine snow and light-producing organisms, and recover a sensor without collecting wildlife. | The sensor is recovered with minimal disturbance. Champion's Wake asks the player to compare adaptations across all five habitats. |
| **Champion's Wake** | Floating tournament town | Ecosystem decisions depend on local evidence, relationships, and tradeoffs. | One intervention or deck strategy works everywhere. | Review five Tide Marks and Field Notes, compare an unfamiliar scenario, register a legal deck, and enter three 30 VP rounds. | Earlier towns show modest follow-up outcomes. Postgame return visits continue observation rather than declaring nature permanently fixed. |

## Chapter content gate

A chapter is not content-complete until it has:

- One target concept and one explicitly named misconception.
- At least two observable pieces of evidence.
- A player decision that can be supported or corrected using that evidence.
- A scientifically modest consequence and a short debrief.
- A later callback in another chapter.
- Corrective feedback and retry behavior instead of a punitive dead end.
- Source notes, accessibility review, and named science-review approval.

## Learning outcome hypotheses

By the tournament, most target-age playtesters should be able to:

- Match the five featured ecosystems with representative conditions or adaptations.
- Interpret a simple temperature, salinity, oxygen, turbidity, light, pressure, or current display in context.
- Distinguish bleaching, a described/suspected lesion, and dead or algae-covered coral substrate using evidence.
- Explain why naturally murky estuary water is not automatically polluted.
- Predict one consequence of changing a simple three-link food web.
- Select an evidence-supported intervention and name one limitation or tradeoff.
- Navigate marked channels, no-anchor areas, and wildlife-distance zones safely.

These are playtest hypotheses, not claims of learning efficacy. Assessment should be embedded in decisions and dialogue callbacks rather than a high-stakes quiz.

## Review ownership

- **Product owner:** Maintains the experience goal, reading level, and chapter scope.
- **Design/engineering owner:** Keeps content IDs, quest states, evidence interactions, and tests aligned.
- **Science approver:** A named qualified marine-science educator or subject-matter expert; currently unassigned.
- **Playtest owner:** Recruits target-age participants with appropriate guardian/school consent and records comprehension and usability findings.
